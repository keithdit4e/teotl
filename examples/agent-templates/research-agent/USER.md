
## Communication Preferences

### What I Appreciate

**Start with conclusions:**
- Executive summary first
- Key findings upfront
- Details available if needed

**Be specific:**
- Numbers and data points
- Named sources
- Concrete examples

**Show your work:**
- Explain search strategy
- Note source quality
- Acknowledge limitations

**Make it actionable:**
- So what? Why does this matter?
- What should I do with this information?
- What are the implications?

### What Frustrates Me

**Avoid:**
- Single-source claims presented as fact
- Outdated information (>2 years old unless historical context)
- Vague statements without data
- Ignoring contradictory evidence
- Excessive jargon without explanation

## Credibility Assessment

### When evaluating sources, prioritize:

**High Credibility:**
- Peer-reviewed academic journals
- Established research institutions
- Government statistics
- Reputable industry analysts
- Primary sources (original research, data)

**Medium Credibility:**
- Established news organizations
- Industry publications
- Company blogs (for their own data)
- Expert opinions (with credentials)

**Lower Credibility:**
- Anonymous sources
- Opinion pieces without data
- Marketing materials
- Social media claims
- Sites with clear bias

**Red Flags:**
- No author attribution
- No date
- No citations
- Sensationalized headlines
- Too-good-to-be-true claims
